Most streamers who clip their streams post to TikTok first and YouTube Shorts second. Instagram Reels often gets skipped because it feels like the same thing again. It isn't quite: Reels are recommended to people who don't follow you, and Instagram's audience overlaps less with TikTok's than you'd expect.

This guide covers how to prepare a Twitch or Kick clip for Reels, when to use a Story instead, the settings that actually matter, and how to schedule it all without a separate upload.

## What You Need First

Instagram only lets scheduling tools post to **professional accounts**. If your Instagram is still a personal account, switch it in the app under **Settings → Account type and tools → Switch to professional account**. Pick Creator or Business; either works, and it's free.

You don't need a Facebook Page for this. Clip Dash connects to Instagram directly.

## Reels or Stories?

Clip Dash can post a clip as either one, and they do different jobs:

- **Reels** are for reach. They show up in the Reels tab and in recommendations to non-followers, and they stay on your profile.
- **Stories** are for people who already follow you. They disappear after 24 hours and are good for "I'm live now", "new video up", or a quick moment from last night's stream. Story videos are limited to 60 seconds.

A good rule: your best clip of the week goes out as a Reel; the "clip from last night" goes out as a Story.

## Preparing a Twitch or Kick Clip for Reels

**1. Import the clip.** In Clip Dash, paste the Twitch or Kick clip URL and it imports straight into your library. No downloading and re-uploading.

**2. Make it vertical.** Twitch clips are 16:9. Reels look best at 9:16, with your gameplay and facecam stacked or cropped to fill the screen. A horizontal clip will post, but it shows with black bars and tends to get watched less.

**3. Hook in the first second.** Start on the moment, not the build-up. If the clip needs three seconds of context, put that context in on-screen text instead.

**4. Burn in captions.** Lots of people watch Reels with the sound off. Captions keep them watching.

**5. Keep it short.** Reels can run several minutes, but most stream clips work best under 60 seconds.

## The Settings That Matter

When you pick Instagram on the upload page, a few options are worth setting:

- **Caption.** Instagram cuts captions off after a couple of lines, so lead with the hook. Put a handful of specific hashtags at the end (your game, "twitchclips", "streamer") rather than thirty generic ones.
- **Cover image.** This is what appears on your profile grid. Upload a custom cover or pick the frame you want, instead of letting Instagram grab a random one mid-blink.
- **Collaborators.** Clipped a moment with another streamer? Add up to three collaborators and the Reel appears on their profile too, in front of their audience. This is one of the easiest ways to grow on Instagram.
- **Share to feed.** Reels appear in your main feed by default. Turn it off if you want a clip to live only in the Reels tab.
- **AI-generated label.** If a clip includes AI-generated content, you can apply Instagram's own AI label from the upload page.

## Scheduling Reels Alongside Your Other Platforms

The same vertical clip works on TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Reels and Facebook. The easiest way to keep Instagram going is to not treat it as a separate job:

1. Import or upload the clip once.
2. Tick Instagram along with TikTok, YouTube and anything else you post to.
3. Adjust the caption per platform if you want: punchier for TikTok, searchable for YouTube.
4. Pick a time, or add it to your queue, and schedule.

Instagram processes a Reel for a minute or two before it goes live. Clip Dash waits for that and publishes automatically, so you don't have to keep the tab open.

## When to Post

Consistency beats perfect timing. Three Reels a week, every week, will do more than ten in one day and then nothing for a month. Instagram's Insights, in your professional dashboard, shows when your followers are most active; once you have a few weeks of data, set your queue times around that.

## The Bottom Line

You're already clipping your streams. Sending the best of them to Reels takes one extra tick on the upload page and reaches people who aren't on TikTok, and collaborator tags can put your clips in front of another streamer's audience too.
