## What a Link in Bio Page Is Actually For

The generic advice is "put all your links in one place." That's true but it undersells the job. For a video creator, the bio link does three specific things:

**It catches traffic from platforms that don't allow links.** TikTok captions can't contain clickable links. Instagram captions can't either. Your bio link is the only escape hatch from those platforms, and it's carrying every conversion you'll ever get from them.

**It routes people who found you on the wrong platform.** Someone finds a 30-second clip of yours on TikTok. The full 40-minute video is on YouTube. Without a bio link, that person has to search your name on YouTube and hope. With one, it's a tap.

**It proves you're active.** A visitor deciding whether to follow you is asking one question: does this person still post? A page with a fresh grid of recent videos answers that instantly. A page with three static buttons and a link to a Twitch stream you last ran in March answers it too, just badly.

That third job is the one nearly every link in bio tool fails, and it fails for a boring reason: those tools have no idea what you posted. They're a page builder. You have to go back and update them manually, and nobody does that consistently.

## Why Creators Pay Twice

Here's the stack a typical video creator ends up with:

| Link in bio page | Linktree Pro, Beacons, Stan | $5–$29/mo |

That's three subscriptions, three logins, and three tools that don't know anything about each other. The scheduler knows exactly what you published and when. The link in bio page — the one thing that most needs that information — never sees it.

The pricing is also structured against you. Most link in bio tools put the things creators actually need behind the paid tier:

- **Click analytics** — usually paid. Without it you have no idea which link is doing the work.
- **Removing the tool's branding** — usually paid.
- **Custom colours and themes** — usually paid.
- **More than a handful of links** — sometimes capped on free.

The free tier exists to be insufficient. That's the business model, and it's a reasonable one when the page *is* the product. It's a bad deal when you're already paying for a tool that could just include it.

## Writing Links That Get Clicked

A few things that consistently move the number:

**Label the destination, not the platform.** "Watch the full 40-minute breakdown" outperforms "YouTube." The visitor doesn't care which platform it's on, they care what they get.

**Keep it under five links.** Every extra option splits attention. If you have eight links, the top one gets fewer clicks than it would if you had three.

**Reorder for what's current.** If you dropped a video this morning, that's link one today. Move it down tomorrow.

**Check the click counts.** A link with near-zero clicks after a month isn't underperforming — it's taking up a slot. Remove it.

## Link in Bio and SEO

Worth knowing, because it's frequently overstated: bio link pages carry `nofollow` on most platforms, so they don't pass meaningful ranking signal to your site. Nobody's ranking a Linktree page for a competitive term.

What they do give you is a stable, indexable page that shows up when someone searches your creator name. If you're building a personal brand, having a page you control appear alongside your social profiles in that search is genuinely useful — it just isn't a link-building strategy, and any tool that sells it as one is overselling.

## When a Paid Tool Still Makes Sense

Being straight about the limits: if you're running commerce through your bio link — selling digital products, taking bookings, gating an email list with a lead magnet, handling payments on the page itself — a dedicated platform like Stan or Beacons does things a scheduler's bundled page doesn't. That's a real product with real features, and paying for it is reasonable.

But if what you need is a clean page, your links, click data, and a current grid of your videos, paying a second subscription for it is money spent on a problem your scheduler already has the data to solve.
