Facebook is easy to forget when you're focused on TikTok and YouTube Shorts, but it still has one of the largest video audiences anywhere, and a lot of it skews older than TikTok's. For streamers and creators, a Facebook Page is a cheap way to reach people who will never open TikTok.

The catch is that posting there by hand is one more upload, one more caption, one more tab. Scheduling fixes that. This guide covers how Facebook video scheduling works, how to handle more than one Page, and how to send the same video to Instagram in the same step.

## Pages, Not Profiles

The first thing to know: scheduling tools can post to **Facebook Pages**, not personal profiles.

Meta closed off third-party posting to personal profiles years ago. Every scheduler that talks to Facebook, Clip Dash included, publishes through a Page you manage. If you've been posting clips from your personal account, this is a good moment to set up a Page for your channel:

- A Page can have followers without you accepting friend requests.
- It keeps your personal account separate from your audience.
- Page videos show up in search and in the Page's Videos tab.

Creating a Page takes a couple of minutes from the Facebook menu (**Pages → Create new Page**). Use your channel name so people can find you.

## Your Options for Scheduling Facebook Videos

**Meta Business Suite (free, built in):**
- Schedules posts and videos to your Pages
- Also handles Instagram if your accounts are linked
- Only covers Meta's own apps, so TikTok, YouTube and the rest are separate jobs

**A cross-platform scheduler:**
- Schedules Facebook alongside TikTok, YouTube, Instagram and others from one upload
- One library of videos instead of a copy per platform
- Useful if an editor or teammate posts for you

If Facebook and Instagram are the only places you post, Business Suite is fine. If you're already uploading the same clip to four or five platforms, adding Facebook to that same upload is the cheaper habit.

## How to Schedule a Facebook Page Video in Clip Dash

1. **Connect Facebook.** In **Settings → Connected Accounts**, connect Facebook and choose which Pages to allow. Every Page you grant is saved as its own account.
2. **Upload or import your video.** Drop in a file, or paste a Twitch or Kick clip URL to import it directly.
3. **Pick the Page.** Under Facebook, tick the Page (or Pages) this video should go to.
4. **Write the title and description.** Facebook shows both, so give the title the hook and use the description for context and a link to your channel.
5. **Add a thumbnail.** Upload a custom thumbnail so the video doesn't open on a random frame.
6. **Pick a time and schedule.** Choose a date and time or add it to your queue. Clip Dash posts it for you, even if your computer is off.

## Running More Than One Facebook Page

Plenty of creators end up with more than one Page: a main channel and a clips channel, a Page per game, or Pages they run for someone else.

Clip Dash treats each Page as a separate account:

- **Connect them all at once.** When you connect Facebook, grant every Page you want to post to. They all show up in your connections.
- **Nothing is preselected.** If you have two or more Pages, Clip Dash doesn't tick one for you. You choose the Page on every post, so a clip meant for your gaming Page never lands on your business Page by accident.
- **Post to several Pages at once.** Tick more than one Page and the same video is scheduled to each of them.

If you later add a new Page on Facebook, reconnect Facebook and grant the new one.

## Post to Facebook and Instagram in One Step

Facebook and Instagram audiences overlap less than you'd think, so most videos are worth sending to both.

When you schedule a video, tick your Facebook Page and your Instagram account together. A few differences worth knowing:

- **Instagram wants vertical.** 9:16 fills the Reels player. Facebook handles both vertical and horizontal well.
- **Captions behave differently.** Facebook shows a title and description; Instagram shows a single caption that gets cut off after a couple of lines, so lead with the hook.
- **Hashtags matter more on Instagram.** On Facebook, a clear title and description do more work than a block of hashtags.

## What to Post to a Facebook Page

The clips that do well on Facebook tend to be ones that make sense without context:

- **Big moments with a clear payoff:** a clutch round, a jump scare, a ridiculous fail.
- **Funny clips with on-screen captions.** Many people scroll Facebook with the sound off.
- **Longer highlights.** Facebook is more forgiving of a two- to five-minute highlight than TikTok is, so it's a good home for "best of the week" compilations.

Inside jokes from chat usually land better on platforms where your regulars already follow you.

## Facebook Page Video Checklist

| Item | Guideline |
|------|-----------|
| Where it posts | A Facebook Page you manage (not a personal profile) |
| Title | Short, with the hook up front |
| Description | Context, plus a link to your stream or channel |
| Thumbnail | Upload a custom one |
| Shape | Vertical or horizontal both work; vertical for cross-posting to Reels |
| Sound | Assume muted; burn in captions |

## The Bottom Line

You're already making the clips. Adding a Facebook Page to the same upload takes a few seconds, reaches an audience that isn't on TikTok, and with multiple Pages handled properly there's no risk of posting to the wrong one.
