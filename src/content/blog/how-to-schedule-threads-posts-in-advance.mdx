Threads has grown faster than almost any social platform in history, and its posting tools have caught up. Since January 2025 you can schedule posts directly in the Threads app, no third-party tool required.

This guide covers how to schedule Threads posts natively, what the official Threads API lets scheduling tools do, and how to fit Threads into a workflow where video is the main event.

## How to Schedule a Threads Post in the App

Threads' built-in scheduler is the simplest option for most people:

1. Open the composer and write your post (text, images or video).
2. Tap the **three-dot menu** in the composer.
3. Tap **Schedule**, then pick a date and time.
4. Tap **Schedule** again to confirm.

Scheduled posts sit in your drafts, where you can edit or delete them before they go out.

**Limits of the native scheduler:**

- You can schedule up to **75 days** ahead.
- **Replies can't be scheduled**, only top-level posts.
- It schedules one post at a time. Useful for a handful of posts, tedious for a full content calendar.

## Scheduling Threads Through the API

Meta has offered a public Threads API since 2024. Tools built on it can publish posts for you, including:

- **Text posts**, single images and videos, and carousels mixing images and videos.
- **Videos up to 5 minutes** and 1 GB, in MP4 or MOV.
- Up to **250 API-published posts per profile** in any 24-hour window. A carousel counts as one post.

To publish on your behalf, a tool needs your permission through Meta's login and has to be approved by Meta for Threads publishing. When you evaluate a Threads scheduler, check that it posts through the official API, not by asking for your password or reposting from Instagram.

## Where Threads Fits in a Video Workflow

For most video creators and streamers, Threads is the conversation layer, not the main stage. The clip lives on TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels; the Threads post is the hot take, the behind-the-scenes note, or the "new video's up" nudge.

A practical split:

- **Schedule the video itself** across your main video platforms from one upload. [Clip Dash](/) does this for YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, Bluesky, X and Pinterest. It doesn't post to Threads today.
- **Schedule the Threads companion post** with Threads' native scheduler, timed a few minutes after the video goes live elsewhere.

That keeps your video pipeline automated while Threads gets copy written for Threads, rather than a caption pasted from TikTok.

## Tips for Threads Posts That Travel

**Write for replies.** Threads rewards conversation. End with a question or a strong opinion rather than a call to "check out my video."

**Lead with the moment, not the link.** A one-line description of the best part of your clip beats "new video out now."

**Keep hashtags to one topic tag.** Threads uses a single topic tag per post, so pick the one that matches the conversation you want to join.

**Check reply controls before you schedule.** Decide who can reply when you write the post.

## The Bottom Line

You no longer need a third-party tool just to schedule a Threads post: the built-in scheduler handles one-off posts well, up to 75 days out. Where tools still earn their place is volume and coordination: publishing the same video everywhere else from one upload, with Threads carrying the conversation around it.
