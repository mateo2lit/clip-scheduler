Most streamers send their clips to TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels, then stop. Pinterest rarely makes the list, which is exactly why it's worth a look. It's one of the few platforms where a clip can keep getting discovered long after the week you posted it.

This guide covers why Pinterest works for gaming clips, how to prepare a Twitch or Kick clip for it, and how to schedule pins without adding another hour to your week.

## Why Pinterest Works Differently

Pinterest behaves more like a search engine than a social feed:

- **People search it.** Pins show up for searches like "Minecraft build ideas" or "Elden Ring boss tips", not just in followers' feeds.
- **Pins have a long shelf life.** A TikTok is mostly done within days. A pin that matches a search can keep surfacing for months.
- **Saves spread your content.** When someone saves your pin to one of their boards, it reaches their followers too.
- **Titles and descriptions are indexed.** What you write decides which searches you show up in.

That makes Pinterest a good home for the clips that answer a question or show off something visual: a build, a strategy, a speedrun trick, a funny moment with a clear title.

## Which Clips to Send to Pinterest

Not every clip fits. The ones that do best are:

- **Searchable moments:** "How I beat [boss] with [build]", "Hidden [game] mechanic", "Best [game] settings".
- **Visual payoffs:** base builds, skins, cosplay, setups, art.
- **Clips that work without sound.** Pins autoplay muted, so the first frame and on-screen text need to carry the hook.

Pure reaction clips and inside jokes from chat usually do better on TikTok, where the audience already knows you.

## Preparing a Twitch or Kick Clip for Pinterest

**1. Grab the clip.** In Clip Dash you can paste a Twitch or Kick clip URL and it imports straight into your library, so there's no downloading and re-uploading.

**2. Go vertical.** Vertical video (9:16) fills the most space in the Pinterest feed. The same vertical cut you make for TikTok and Shorts works here.

**3. Add on-screen text.** A short caption burned into the first second ("This boss has one weakness") does the job sound usually does.

**4. Write a searchable title.** Use the words people would type: the game, the thing you're showing, and the outcome. "Elden Ring: easy Malenia cheese with bleed build" beats "LMAO 😂".

**5. Write a real description.** Two or three sentences that expand on the title, with the game name and topic. Hashtags matter less on Pinterest than clear keywords.

## Choosing the Right Board

Every pin lives on a board, and board names are part of how Pinterest understands your content. Create boards by game or topic ("Minecraft Builds", "Valorant Tips", "Stream Highlights") rather than one catch-all "Clips" board.

When you schedule a pin in Clip Dash, you pick the board from your Pinterest account's list, the same way you'd pick a playlist on YouTube.

## Scheduling Pins Alongside Your Other Platforms

The easiest way to keep Pinterest going is to not treat it as a separate job:

1. Import or upload the clip once.
2. Select Pinterest along with TikTok, YouTube, Instagram and whatever else you post to.
3. Write the Pinterest title and description (search-style), and keep your punchier captions for TikTok.
4. Pick the board and a time, then schedule.

Because pins keep working for months, you don't need to post them at exactly the right hour. Consistency matters more than timing: a few pins a week beats a burst of twenty and then nothing.

## Pinterest Video Specs to Know

| Spec | Guideline |
|------|-----------|
| File types | MP4, MOV, M4V |
| Max file size | 2 GB |
| Best shape | Vertical (9:16 or 2:3) |
| Sound | Autoplays muted; use on-screen text |

## The Bottom Line

You're already making the clips. Sending the searchable, visual ones to Pinterest costs a couple of minutes per clip, and those pins can keep bringing new viewers to your channel long after the stream ended.
